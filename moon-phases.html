THE LUNAR CYCLE

Phases of the Moon

The Moon changes its appearance throughout the month. Discover why we see different illuminated shapes as it travels around Earth.

EARTH · MOON ORBIT · LUNAR CYCLE

Why Does the Moon Change Shape?

The Moon does not produce its own visible light. Sunlight illuminates half of the Moon, and as the Moon orbits Earth, we see different portions of that illuminated half. This creates the lunar phases.

EIGHT MAIN PHASES

One Lunar Cycle

PHASE 01

New Moon

The Moon's near side is mostly unilluminated from Earth's viewpoint.

PHASE 02

Waxing Crescent

A small illuminated crescent becomes visible and grows each night.

PHASE 03

First Quarter

About half of the Moon's visible disk appears illuminated.

PHASE 04

Waxing Gibbous

More than half of the visible disk is lit, approaching Full Moon.

PHASE 05

Full Moon

The near side of the Moon appears almost completely illuminated.

PHASE 06

Waning Gibbous

The illuminated portion begins shrinking after the Full Moon.

PHASE 07

Last Quarter

About half of the visible disk is illuminated again, on the opposite side.

PHASE 08

Waning Crescent

A thin crescent remains before the cycle returns to New Moon.

Did you know? The complete lunar phase cycle takes about 29.5 days. The Moon's changing phases are not caused by Earth's shadow; Earth's shadow causes a lunar eclipse.
